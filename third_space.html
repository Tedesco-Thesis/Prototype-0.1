about.htm

what this is

a synthesis experiment, not a conclusion. each room tests one design proposition about what an internet built around reciprocity might feel like, on its own terms — the data room asks for something back, the correspondence room asks for time and commitment, and the signed-work room asks for visible labor instead of a market.

this doesn't argue reciprocity is simply better than optimization. it's a way of asking what gets exchanged for what, and letting that be felt through use rather than stated as an answer.

what this prototype is testing
QUESTION: what might an internet feel like if it were built around reciprocity rather than optimization, across identity, communication, and authorship at once?
INTERVENTION: three separate design propositions (from prototypes 01, 02, and 04's territory) are combined into one inhabitable place instead of three isolated demos, with a live "your trace" panel on the home page that reflects activity across all three rooms.
TRADEOFF: a synthesis is more atmospheric and easier to feel as a whole, but harder to isolate — it's less clear which specific proposition is doing the work if someone likes or dislikes the experience.
OBSERVATION: the trace panel is the actual test of coherence — read its closing line once you've used all three rooms, and notice whether it reads as one relationship with this place or three unrelated favors.
LIMITATION: this is deliberately the least conclusive prototype in the series — it should not be read as the thesis's answer, only as one way several propositions might coexist.
